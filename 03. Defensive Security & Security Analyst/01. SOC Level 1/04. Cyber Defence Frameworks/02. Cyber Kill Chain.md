## Task 1: Introduction

The **Cyber Kill Chain** is a cybersecurity framework developed by Lockheed Martin in 2011. It explains the 7 stages hackers may follow to carry out a cyberattack.


### 7 Phases of the Cyber Kill Chain

1. **Reconnaissance**: Gather information about the target.
2. **Weaponization**: Create malware or prepare an attack tool.
3. **Delivery**: Send the malicious file or link to the victim.
4. **Exploitation**: Exploit a vulnerability to gain access.
5. **Installation**: Install malware on the victim's system.
6. **Command & Control (C2)**: Remotely communicate with and control the compromised system.
7. **Actions on Objectives**: Achieve the attacker's goal, such as stealing data or encrypting files.


### Learning Objectives

In this room, we will learn about each phase of the Cyber Kill Chain Framework, the advantages and disadvantages of the traditional Cyber Kill Chain. 

**Outcome**:

As a result, we will be ready to recognize different phases or stages of the attack carried out by an adversary and be able to break the "kill chain."

## Task 2: Reconnaissance 

<img width="865" height="800" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/4f69e756-2e74-4289-9241-440afd7ff6f1" />


**Reconnaissance** is the first stage of a cyberattack. Attackers research and gather information about their target before launching an attack.

#### What is information DV DO Attacker Collect?

- **Network information**: IP addresses, domains, and servers.
- **Employee information**: Names, emails, and job roles.
- **System information**: Software, technologies, and potential weaknesses.
- **Business information**: Company activities and processes.


#### What Is OSINT?

**[OSINT (Open-Source Intelligence)](https://www.varonis.com/blog/what-is-osint/)** means collecting information from publicly available sources.

Common OSINT source include:

- **Search Engine**: Google, Binng
- **Social Mmedi**: Facebook, LinkedIn
- **Website and Blogs**: Company information and new.
- **Public Record**: Publick Accessible database.
- **WHOIS**: Domain registeration information.
- **Online forum**: Public disclossion.



### Reconnaissance Types

Reconnaissance has **two main types**:

| Type              | Meaning                                                             | Examples                                         |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Passive Recon** | Gathering information without directly interacting with the target. | WHOIS, social media, Google searches             |
| **Active Recon**  | Gathering information by directly interacting with the target.      | Port scanning, banner grabbing, probing services |


#### What Is Email Harvesting?

**Email harvesting** means collecting email addresses from public or other available sources.

Attackers may use collected emails to launch **phishing attacks** that attempt to steal passwords or sensitive information.

**Common OSINT Tools:**
- [theHarvester](https://github.com/laramies/theHarvester): Collects emails, names, subdomains, IP addresses, and URLs.
- [Hunter.io](https://hunter.io): Finds email addresses associated with a company's domain.
- [OSINT Framework](https://osintframework.com/): Provides a collection of OSINT tools organized by category.

## Task 3: Weaponizatio0n

<img width="865" height="800" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/1cbdf374-2a31-4b45-beef-f54eb9b22050" />


**Weaponization** is the stage where attackers use information collected during reconnaissance to **create** or **prepare malware**, **exploits**, and **malicious payloads** malware refer to [DarkWeb](https://www.kaspersky.com/resource-center/threats/deep-web) for an attack.


#### How Do Attackers Prepare?
- **Use reconnaissance data**: Study information collected about the target.
- **Create malware**: Use automated tools to generate malicious software.
- **Buy malware**: Some attackers purchase malware from the [Dark Web](https://www.kaspersky.com/resource-center/threats/deep-web).
- **Develop custom malware**: Advanced attackers, such as APT groups, create unique malware to avoid security detection.


**Some terminology:**

- **Malware**: Software designed to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to a computer.
- **Exploit**: Code that takes advantage of a security vulnerability.
- **Payload**: Malicious code that runs on a victim's system.


#### Common Weaponization Tactics
- **Malicious Office Documents**: Create Word or Excel files containing harmful macros or VBA scripts.
- **Infected USB Drives**: Place malware on USB drives and distribute them to potential victims.
- **Command and Control (C2)**: Prepare infrastructure to remotely control infected computers or deliver more malware.
- **Backdoors**: Prepare hidden access methods that can bypass normal security controls.
- **Phishing Templates**: Create fake emails, websites, or app permission requests that look legitimate to trick victims.

## Task 4: Delivery

<img width="865" height="800" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b1ce5c97-44ab-4a92-afa7-171b96e1a6af" />


**Delivery** is the third stage of the Cyber Kill Chain. It is when attackers **send malware or malicious payloads to their targets**.

#### **Common Delivery Methods**

1. **Phishing Email** – Send fake emails containing malicious links or attachments to trick victims.

- **Spear Phishing:** Targets a specific person or organization.

2. **USB Drop Attack** – Leave infected USB drives in public places, such as coffee shops, parking lots, or offices, hoping someone will plug them into a computer.

3. **Watering Hole Attack** – Compromise websites frequently visited by a specific group to deliver malware or redirect visitors to malicious websites.

- **Drive-by Download:** Malware is downloaded through a compromised or malicious website, sometimes without the user's knowledge.

## Task 5: Exploitation


<img width="865" height="800" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ace8c620-2f3e-417f-bd6f-59ff6369342d" />


**Exploitation** is the fourth stage of the Cyber Kill Chain. It happens when attackers **execute malicious code or exploit security vulnerabilities to compromise a target system**.

#### **Common Exploitation Techniques**

1. **Malicious Macros**: Run harmful scripts inside Office documents, potentially executing ransomware.
2. **Zero-Day Exploits**: Take advantage of previously unknown or unpatched vulnerabilities.
3. **Known CVEs**: Exploit publicly documented security vulnerabilities that have not been patched.

#### **What Happens After Exploitation?**

- **Privilege Escalation:** Gain higher permissions, such as administrator access.
- **Lateral Movement:** Move from one compromised computer to other systems within the network.

#### **Signs of Exploitation**

1. **Unexpected Processes**: Unknown or suspicious programs start running.
2. **Registry Changes**: Unusual modifications to Windows registry settings.
3. **New Services**:: Suspicious services appear on the system.
4. **Suspicious Commands**: Unusual command-line activity appears in system logs.

#### **Simple Example**

Megatron sends a malicious Word document to an employee. The employee enables a harmful macro, which executes malicious code and compromises the computer.

## Task 6: Installation

<img width="865" height="800" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ffdcda63-9193-40d5-935f-51f7862ff32c" />


**Installation** is the fifth stage of the Cyber Kill Chain. Attackers install malware or backdoors to **maintain access to a compromised system**, even after restarting or losing their connection.

#### **Common Installation Techniques**

1. **Web Shell**: A malicious script (PHP, ASP, JSP) installed on a web server to maintain remote access.
2. **Backdoor (Meterpreter)**: A tool that allows attackers to remotely access and control a compromised computer.
3. **Windows Services**:: Create or modify services to automatically execute malicious programs.
4. **Registry Run Keys / Startup Folder**: Configure malware to run automatically when a user logs in.
5. **[Timestomping](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1070/006/)**: Change file timestamps to hide malicious activity and make files appear legitimate.


#### **Simple Example**

**Megatron** compromises a Windows computer and installs a backdoor that automatically starts when the computer restarts.

This allows him to reconnect without repeating the initial attack.

## Task 7: Command & Control

<img width="865" height="800" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/30d2883d-1fd5-4b83-8766-1f8032cbb06d" />


**Command & Control (C2)** is the sixth stage of the Cyber Kill Chain. It allows attackers to **remotely communicate with and control an infected computer** through a C2 server.

#### **How Does C2 Work?**

- **Malware Infection**: The victim's computer is infected with malware.
- **C2 Connection**: The malware connects to the attacker's server.
- **C2 Beaconing**: The infected computer regularly communicates with the C2 server.
- **Remote Control**: The attacker sends commands to the infected computer.

#### **Common C2 Communication Methods**

- **HTTP (Port 80)**: Uses normal web traffic to communicate with the C2 server.
- **HTTPS (Port 443)**: Uses encrypted web traffic to help hide malicious communication.
- **DNS Tunneling**: Uses DNS requests to secretly communicate with the attacker's server.
- **IRC**: An older communication method that attackers previously used for C2.

 
> **C2 Server:** A server attackers use to control infected computers.
>
> **C2 Beaconing:** Repeated communication between malware and a C2 server.
>
> **DNS Tunneling:** Hiding communication inside DNS traffic.

##### **Simple Example**

Megatron infects a computer with malware. The malware regularly contacts his C2 server, allowing him to send commands remotely.

## Task 8: Actions on Objective (Exfiltration)


<img width="865" height="800" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/7f57bb9c-514a-42e1-ac31-76fc6386f7b1" />



**Actions on Objectives** is the final stage of the Cyber Kill Chain. Attackers **achieve their main goals after successfully compromising a system**.

#### **Common Attacker Actions**

- **Credential Theft** – Steal usernames and passwords.
- **Privilege Escalation** – Gain higher permissions, such as administrator access.
- **Internal Reconnaissance** – Explore internal systems to find weaknesses.
- **Lateral Movement** – Move between computers within the network.
- **Data Exfiltration** – Steal sensitive data and send it outside the company.
- **Delete Backups** – Remove backups and shadow copies to prevent easy recovery.
- **Data Destruction** – Delete, overwrite, or corrupt important files.

#### **Important Terms**

- **Credentials:** Usernames and passwords.
- **Privilege Escalation:** Gaining higher access permissions.
- **Lateral Movement:** Moving from one compromised system to another.
- **Data Exfiltration:** Stealing and transferring sensitive information.
- **Shadow Copies:** Backup snapshots of files or storage volumes used for recovery.

## Task 10:: Conclusion


<img width="1141" height="827" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/2e2bb564-9e10-4bfd-a7f0-dac6a1e111fb" />



The **Cyber Kill Chain** helps cybersecurity professionals understand and prevent attacks, but **it is not perfect**.

#### **Main Limitations**

1. **Outdated Framework**: Created in 2011 and does not fully address modern cyber threats.
2. **Focuses on Malware**: Mainly designed to protect networks against external malware attacks.
3. **Advanced Attacks**: Modern attackers use multiple techniques (TTPs) to bypass security.
4. **Changing Indicators**: Attackers change IP addresses and file hashes to avoid detection.
5. **Insider Threats**: Does not effectively identify threats from employees or other authorized users.

#### **Better Security Approaches**

Combine the Cyber Kill Chain with:

- **MITRE ATT&CK**: Helps identify and understand attackers' tactics and techniques.
- **Unified Kill Chain**: Provides a more comprehensive view of cyberattacks.
- **AI-Based Security Tools**: Help detect suspicious activity and unusual behavior.
